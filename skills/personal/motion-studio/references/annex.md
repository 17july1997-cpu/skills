## A. Idea Brief checklist

The grill rounds end when every line has an answer or a stated assumption. Each item is tagged with the round it usually comes from.

1. **Film in one line:** what it is and for whom. *(R1)*
2. **Audience,** and the one feeling or action at the end. *(R1)*
3. **Length, primary format, fps,** and any extra formats. *(R1, R3)*
4. **Story shape:** for example problem then product then proof then CTA; a step-by-step journey; a showreel of techniques; one-shape morph; a narrative. *(R1)*
5. **Hook:** the single most striking image in the first 2 seconds. *(R2)*
6. **Look:**
   - which brand kit;
   - the reference, or "the house look";
   - texture and grounds;
   - banned looks specific to this video. *(R2)*
7. **Real assets:**
   - product UI, as screenshots, source frames or a URL;
   - logos;
   - footage;
   - data and numbers, each with its source. *(R2)*
8. **Copy:** must-say lines, must-avoid lines, and the CTA or end card. *(R2)*
9. **Sound:**
   - the music feel and BPM, or a supplied track;
   - SFX density;
   - voice on or off, with provider and script owner. *(R3)*
10. **Delivery:** where it ships (deck, LinkedIn, X, site, email), the deadline, and who sees it first. *(R3)*
11. **Constraints:** budget for paid APIs, legal or brand restrictions, competitor names to avoid. *(R3)*
12. **Boards (route E):** camera on or off, the sync mode (words only, voice sync, voice + words), where the voice
    comes from (your recording, an editor export, generated), and the anchor picture if the film has one. *(R3, 4c)*

## B. Question banks (worked examples; adapt, never paste)

Every option needs a `description` saying what the video becomes and what it costs. The recommended option comes first. The **(R)** below marks the usual recommendation, but re-derive it from the evidence every time.

### B1. Recreate or improve an existing video (source MP4 supplied)

**Round 1, the idea:**

- **"What should the new version do better than the original?"**
  - Same story, far better motion and sound **(R)**, when the source copy is approved.
  - Tighter story and shorter.
  - New angle for a new audience.
  - Same everything, only polish.
- **"Who is it for first?"** Options come from the source's own framing. For example, for a partner walkthrough:
  - the partner's team, already signed;
  - prospects;
  - shoppers;
  - internal.
- **"Length and format?"**
  - Keep the source length at 16:9 **(R)**.
  - A 30 to 40s cut-down.
  - 9:16 for social.
  - Both from one timeline.
- **"How should steps change on screen?"**
  - One continuous camera moving through the journey **(R)** for step-based sources.
  - Hard cuts on downbeats.
  - A morphing container, one shape that never cuts.
  - Keep the slide structure but animate within it.

**Round 2, the look and the material:**

- **"The hook?"**
  - Open on the most striking real UI moment.
  - Open on the problem in 5 words.
  - Open on the co-brand lockup.
  - Open on a number.
- **"Look?"**
  - The source's own look, executed properly **(R)** when it is on-brand.
  - The brand kit's dark mode.
  - A named reference from `refs/`.
  - A new look, with a reference to follow.
- **"Real UI?"**
  - Crops from the source frames **(R)** when they are high resolution.
  - Fresh screenshots from the live site.
  - Both.
  - Rebuild as stylised cards. Warn that this risks inventing UI.
- **"End card?"**
  - Co-brand lockup with the URL **(R)**.
  - A CTA line.
  - A loop back to the opening frame.
  - The logo only.

**Round 3, sound and delivery:**

- **"Music?"**
  - Synthesized, calm, around 100 to 110 BPM **(R)** for explainers.
  - Energetic at 120 to 128 BPM.
  - A supplied track.
  - SFX only.
- **"Voice?"**
  - None, with on-screen copy carrying it **(R)** when the source has full copy.
  - A Fish Audio voiceover.
  - The user's cloned voice.
  - Captions only.
- **"Where does it ship?"**
  - In a partner deck or call.
  - LinkedIn.
  - Email.
  - The website.
- **"Anything that must not appear?"** Use options that come from the source, for example:
  - competitor names;
  - prices;
  - the partner's customer data;
  - none.

### B2. Product launch or brand reel (URL supplied)

- **Round 1:**
  - Goal: launch announcement, feature explainer, always-on brand reel, or ad.
  - Length: 15, 20, 30 or 60s.
  - Format: 16:9, 9:16, 1:1, or all three.
  - Shape: problem, product, 3 features, proof, CTA **(R)**; a showreel of techniques; a one-shape UI morph; a story.
- **Round 2:**
  - Hook: the problem in 5 words of kinetic type **(R)**.
  - Reference: a whatships.com pick, the brand's past video, or none.
  - Assets: capture from the URL with Playwright **(R)**, supplied screenshots, or Figma exports.
  - The proof number, which must come from the user or the site.
- **Round 3:**
  - Music BPM.
  - Voice: Fish Audio, cloned, or none.
  - Mascot or character: yes or no.
  - Channel.

### B3. Explainer of a process or concept (notes or a doc supplied)

- **Round 1:**
  - Audience expertise.
  - The one takeaway.
  - Length.
  - Format.
- **Round 2:**
  - Visual metaphor: diagram builds **(R)**, a character, a UI walkthrough, or kinetic type.
  - Route hint: maths content goes to Manim.
  - Brand.
  - Captions.
- **Round 3:**
  - Voice: on **(R)**, because explainers usually need it.
  - Music feel.
  - Chapters.
  - Delivery.

### B4. Showreel or creative piece (no product)

- **Round 1:**
  - Subject: techniques, a history, a mood or a story.
  - Length.
  - Format.
  - Style family: flat graphic, editorial, hand-drawn, 3D or retro.
- **Round 2:**
  - Reference library.
  - Palette constraint.
  - Type constraint.
  - Character: yes or no.
- **Round 3:**
  - Music: synthesized genre, or supplied.
  - Pace.
  - Loop: yes or no.
  - Channel.

### B5. YouTube explainer (talking head or narrated; route E)

- **Round 1:**
  - The one idea the viewer can retell: proposed from the transcript **(R)**, supplied by you, the video title's promise, two candidates to pick from.
  - Who is watching: smart beginner **(R)**, practitioner, decision-maker, mixed.
  - Camera: talking head bottom-right **(R)**, narrated with no face, face only in hook and outro, screen recording plus boards.
  - Length: the final cut's length **(R)**, a tighter cut first, a 60 s Short from the same plan, both.
- **Round 2:**
  - The anchor picture: the one the transcript keeps returning to **(R)**, a timeline, a map, none.
  - Board density: the budget in `brief-method.md` **(R)**, denser (a board every 15 s), lighter (key moments only), boards only where numbers appear.
  - Look: the brand kit themed onto the boards **(R)**, the style step with 3 directions, a named reference, the default theme (warn: template look).
  - Honesty tags: on where the script hedges **(R)**, off, EXAMPLE only, ask per board.
- **Round 3:**
  - Sync: voice sync from your final take **(R)**, voice + kinetic words, words only, generated voice then sync.
  - Sound: voice only **(R)**, voice with a ducked bed, voice with sparse SFX on stamps, both.
  - Extras: chapters + end screen + thumbnail **(R)**, chapters only, plus a Short, none.
  - Delivery: one master with the camera composited **(R)**, board track for the editor, both, board clips per board.

### B6. Proposal, pitch or results film (no presenter; route E)

- **Round 1:**
  - Who decides after watching: the client's buyer **(R)**, an internal approver, investors, the team.
  - The one action at the end: reply yes to the proposal **(R)**, book a call, approve budget, share it on.
  - Length: 45-60 s **(R)**, 30 s, 90 s, a 15 s teaser plus the full film.
  - Format: 16:9 for the deck or call **(R)**, 1:1 for email and LinkedIn, 9:16, all from one plan.
- **Round 2:**
  - Story shape: problem, plan, proof, ask **(R)**, before vs after, three numbers, the plan as a timeline.
  - The proof number, with its source: from the doc **(R)**, from the user, from a past result, none (cut proof).
  - Look: the client's or the user's brand kit **(R)**, co-branded, the style step, a named reference.
  - End card: the ask with a date **(R)**, the logo lockup, a contact, a loop to the opening.
- **Round 3:**
  - Timing: words only at reading pace **(R)**, a voiceover with sync, voice + words, generated voice.
  - Music: synthesized, calm, 100-110 BPM with cuts on downbeats **(R)**, energetic, supplied, none.
  - Where it plays: in a live call **(R)** (no captions needed), sent cold by email (on-screen copy carries it), LinkedIn, the website.
  - Must not appear: the client's competitors **(R)**, prices, other clients' names, none.

## C. Critique rubric (score 1 to 10 each, every round)

| Axis | 8+ looks like | Typical failures |
|---|---|---|
| Hook (first 2s) | A striking, specific image lands within 2s | Logo first, slow fade-in, an empty frame |
| Readability at 360px | Every line legible on the `phone.png` sheet, held long enough to read (about 0.3s per word, 1.2s minimum) | Small type, text on busy UI, copy held 0.5s |
| Motion quality | Springs with mass, nothing slides linearly, blur on fast moves, no pops | Linear tweens, stepped ghosts at low `--sub`, jumps between shots |
| Variety | Something new every 2 to 4s, no repeated move three times in a row | The same fade-up on every element |
| Composition | Clear focal point, intentional negative space, safe margins | Centered everything, crowding, corner labels |
| Brand accuracy | Real logo, real fonts, palette from the kit, one accent, voice rules kept | Fallback fonts, an off-palette accent, a typed logo |
| Sound sync | Scene changes on downbeats, SFX on actions (onsets within 1 frame) | Hits drifting off picture, SFX at t=0, cues masked |
| Sync (route E and any word-synced film) | Every element lands 0.05-2 s after the word that names it, never before; no stretch over 5 s with nothing new while the voice talks; text up for its read time | A title that gives away the punchline, everything at t=0, labels leaving before they can be read, a misheard anchor landing nothing |

For a recreation, score the source on the same axes first (the baseline) and report both.

Rules for the shot list:

- Scene changes sit on downbeats.
- Every on-screen fact has a source.
- Copy is final, with no em dashes.

## F. Failure modes (recorded from the sources and from builds)

| Failure | What to do |
|---|---|
| "Brief contagion": the one-liner showreel prompt produces reels that all rhyme | Use it only to test the engine. Real videos come from the grill. |
| Opus defaults to its own zero-dependency route when no framework is named | Name the route explicitly from the matrix. |
| GPU text rasterisation makes renders differ | Run determinism checks with `--cpu`, and compare decoded frames. |
| Stepped ghost copies on fast moves | Raise `--sub` to 8, or up to 24 on whip moves. |
| Blank frames in dark-to-light transitions, and strobing on fast moves | Ease the transition, and check the strip around it. |
| Full-app shots unreadable on a phone | Zoom into the region that matters. Never show the whole app for long. |
| Copy readable for only 0.05s | Hold each line for its read time (rubric, Readability). |
| A short, transient-heavy mix stalls below target loudness | `mix.sh` already limits before loudnorm. Check the `I:` line. |
| ffprobe reports a stream duration with a trailing comma, or N/A | `mix.sh` already strips it and falls back to the container duration. |
| The HyperFrames init checks or installs skills globally | Always set `HYPERFRAMES_SKIP_SKILLS=1`. |
| The Remotion scaffold does not install its dependencies | Run `npm install` inside the project before rendering. |
| The model cannot hear the mix | Verify SFX by onsets on the stem, and ask the user to listen. |
| A logo is invisible on its own brand colour (a lime shield on a lime card) | Use the official alternate variant (for example the black shield). Never recolour a logo file yourself. |
| A retailer logo carries file padding, so the lockup sits off-centre | Trim it to its bounding box with PIL, then build the lockup with equal gaps either side of the x. |
| The installed `hyperframes` skill claims to be the mandatory entry point for any video | `motion-studio` stays the front door. Framework skills are tools used only after the route decision. |
| A supplied music track from a stock site | Check its licence note. Stock licences are per account, so flag external use. |
| A character overlaps captions or cards | Give it a fixed home (lower left, on the path line) and keep content above it. Check every station's still. |
| Scores plateau below 8 | Change the approach. After 2 flat rounds, show the blocking frame and ask. |
| A catalog board pasted into a video plays its 12 s preview loop instead of landing on words | Rebuild it on `board-shell.html` with `data-move` + `data-cue`; catalog boards are references. |
| An anchor lands nothing because Whisper misheard a name or number | Read the low-confidence words `transcribe_words.py` prints; fix the words TSV, never the script; re-sync. |
| The 16 kHz `V/sync/voice.wav` ends up in the final | It is for timing only. The mix uses the original take at 48 kHz. |
| A `.sticker` stretches into a tall line | Give it an explicit height and fill. |
| Captions and a handwritten note collide bottom-left | Keep x60-1180, y930-1020 free with captions on; `check_board.mjs` flags collisions. |
| The talking-head master is shorter than the board track | The camera take ended early; the export keeps the boards running (`eof_action=pass`) and the frame-count check catches any loss. |
| Every board looks like the source repo's demo | The default theme shipped. Theme from the brand kit or run the style step (`tokens-default.md`). |

## R. Install and relocation

The skill finds the studio itself (the resolver in `SKILL.md`): `$MOTION_STUDIO` if set, otherwise `~/Desktop/WORK/SKILLS/Motion Graphics`, then `~/loxa-agent-resources/motion-studio`. No repackaging is needed when the studio moves.

- **Not installed.** Get `motion-studio.zip` from the AI Enablement page in Notion, unzip it, and follow its `install.md` (or run `bash install.sh` inside it). It follows the Loxa "How to Package a Skill" standard: the skill goes to `~/.claude/skills/motion-studio/` and the studio to `~/loxa-agent-resources/motion-studio/`. It then runs the engine tests and prints `INSTALLED`.
- **Kept somewhere else.** Add `export MOTION_STUDIO="<path to the studio>"` to `~/.zshrc`, open a new terminal, and start a new Claude Code session.
- **Updating.** Run the new zip's `bash install.sh --update`. It keeps your `videos/` and backs up the old studio and skill first.
- **Check.** `engine/tests/run-tests.sh <scratch dir>` in the studio must print `ALL PASS`.

Maintainer note (the canonical skill lives in `~/Desktop/WORK/SKILLS/motion-studio/`): after editing it, repackage with `~/Desktop/WORK/SKILLS/package-skill.sh motion-studio ~/Desktop/WORK/SKILLS/motion-studio`, mirror it to the other four stores, and rebuild the team zip with `team-bundle/source/build-bundle.sh` in the studio.
